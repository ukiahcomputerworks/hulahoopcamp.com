Your way to the forest

Arrival Guide

Prepare for a rural, off-grid weekend and arrive with enough daylight to settle in comfortably.

Watch the road walkthrough and save the route before leaving reliable service. Confirm current conditions with Kelly before you drive.

Before the pavement ends

Come ready for the road, the dark, and off-grid life.

The camp archive is wonderfully consistent about one thing: preparation protects your time in the forest. Town is not close, the land gets truly dark, and shared systems work only when everyone arrives ready to use them.

01
Save the route before you leave service

Historic directions describe the homestead as 4.3 miles up Shimmins Ridge Road, about 15 minutes from the freeway. Watch the video, screenshot key turns, and save Kelly's current route rather than relying on an old map pin.

02
Give the road time and daylight

The archived guide describes a rough first mile with several tight turns, followed by a more agreeable dirt road. Arrive before dark, drive slowly, and come with reliable tires. Ask Kelly about current road and weather conditions.

03
Bring enough food and drinking water

There are no food vendors, and the nearest store was historically about half an hour away by dirt road. Pack meals, snacks, a cooler with ice, and large water containers for the full stay. Bring a reusable bottle for any refill station Kelly confirms.

04
Cook and store food together

Past camps centered meals at one community kitchen with shared stoves, a wash basin, and morning hot water. Keep food and coolers sealed and use the current central storage and cooking plan. Do not make a separate campsite cooking fire.

05
Plan for darkness and limited power

The property is off-grid. Bring a headlamp or flashlight, spare batteries, and charged power banks for phones, cameras, speakers, and illuminated props. Treat any charging station or generator access as limited unless Kelly confirms otherwise.

06
Pack for a forest weekend

Bring your tent and sleeping gear, tarp, chair, layers for warm days and chilly nights, and rain coverage. Use biodegradable soap, sunscreen, and bug repellent to help protect the watershed, then pack all trash back out with you.

A living guide: These preparations come from past HulaHoopCamp materials. Route conditions, weather, water, kitchen equipment, fire restrictions, and available services can change. Kelly's current instructions take priority.
